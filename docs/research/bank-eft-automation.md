# TTLI_LMS — Direct Bank/EFT Payment Automation Research

**Scope:** Whether TTLI's current manual EFT flow (buyer transfers via their own bank, uploads proof, finance approves by hand) can be automated via direct South African bank integrations or payment rails — PayShap, bank-native "Instant EFT" products, each major bank's own business/merchant API, and the state of Open Banking in South Africa — and, if so, what is actually worth building now versus deferring.

**Audience:** Engineering/Product. This is a build-prioritization call, not a compliance one — though PayShap Request-to-Pay or any new bank/PSP integration would be a new third-party data flow worth a POPIA glance from whoever owns `docs/04_SECURITY_AND_COMPLIANCE.md` if it's ever picked up.

**Method note:** The codebase context section below was read directly from this repository, not sourced from the web. Every other claim is web-sourced and cited inline per section; sources were checked for currency (South African fintech/regulatory context, checked as of August 2026) rather than assumed from general knowledge, since payment-rail limits and bank product names change.

## Codebase context (read directly, not web-sourced)

The current EFT flow, confirmed from `apps/api/src/services/orders.py` and `apps/api/src/routers/orders.py`:

- `checkout_eft` transitions the order to `eft_pending_proof` and generates a unique `payment_reference` (format `EFT-{order_id.hex[12:22]}`)
- `submit_proof` accepts an uploaded, virus-scanned image/PDF and transitions to `eft_pending_approval`
- `approve_eft`/`reject_eft` require the `payment:approve` permission — a human judgment call, no automated path. `docs/02_DATA_MODEL.md` §6.3 states this explicitly: **"There is no auto-approval path for EFT."**
- Card payments already use a clean abstraction, `services/payments/base.py`'s `PaymentProvider` Protocol: `initiate_checkout` (redirect), `verify_signature` (pure), `confirm_with_provider` (live anti-forgery round-trip), `parse_webhook` (normalizes to `WebhookResult`). Implemented once, for Payfast (`services/payments/payfast.py`) — written to spec but **never verified against a live account**; no sandbox credentials exist yet, which is a named Phase 0 blocker.
- `docs/02_DATA_MODEL.md` §12.4 already specifies a **daily "EFT ageing alert" job** that flags approvals pending &gt;48 hours — this is the real, built-in signal for "the manual queue has become a backlog," not a guess.
- `docs/05_COMMERCIAL.md` gives concrete pricing that turned out to be decisive (see below): Individual tiers (R950–R9,500, card-default, EFT is an optional ➕ add-on) vs. Team (R18,500–R65,000, EFT/invoice ✅ default), Corporate (R75,000–R150,000+, PO/invoice), Enterprise (R195,000–R550,000+, contract terms). EFT is concentrated in exactly the higher-value B2B tiers.

## 1. PayShap — verified

PayShap is real and operating at national scale: launched March 2023 by BankservAfrica (South Africa's clearing house) under the SARB's Vision 2025 programme, officially the "Rapid Payments Programme" (RPP). It clears in under 10 seconds, runs 24/7, and had processed over R400 billion / hundreds of millions of transactions by early 2026. It supports three product shapes: pay-by-account, pay-by-proxy (a "ShapID," typically a phone number), and **Request to Pay (RTP)**.

RTP is a genuine automation opportunity and matches what the task asked about: a merchant sends a payment request to a customer's ShapID or account; the customer approves inside their own banking app (no credentials shared with the merchant or any intermediary); on approval, **both payer and merchant get real-time confirmation**, delivered to the merchant as a webhook/API callback through whichever PSP mediates it. This is not merely a smoother buyer experience — it is real programmatic confirmation.

**Access model:** Businesses do not integrate with BankservAfrica directly. RTP is accessed through PSPs already operating in South Africa — Ozow, Netcash, Peach Payments, and Stitch all have shipped PayShap RTP products. Peach's guide cites SME pricing around "R1.50 + R1.50" per transaction (flat-fee, not percentage) — cheap by design since PayShap targets low-value, high-frequency payments. Ozow states RTP is available "with no additional development needed" for merchants already integrated with Ozow.

**The decisive caveat for TTLI specifically:** PayShap's scheme-wide cap is **R50,000 per transaction** (raised from R3,000 in October 2024; no further increase found as of mid-2026), and individual banks set their own limits within that — some far lower (Capitec: 2×R500/day; Standard Bank: R5,000 in one source; Nedbank/Investec: up to R50,000). Cross-referencing against TTLI's own pricing in `docs/05_COMMERCIAL.md`: this comfortably covers Individual tiers and Team's 5-seat package, partially covers Team's 10-seat package, but **Corporate (R75,000–R150,000+) and Enterprise (R195,000–R550,000+) deals sit structurally above the cap** — exactly the tiers where EFT is the default (✅), not optional, payment method. PayShap RTP cannot be the answer for TTLI's highest-value EFT customers; at best it addresses the smaller end of the EFT population, most of which already prefers card.

Sources: [Netcash — PayShap for Business](https://netcash.co.za/blog/accelerate-your-business-with-payshap-embracing-instant-payments-and-cost-efficiency/), [Stitch — state of PayShap in 2026](https://stitch.money/blog/real-time-payments-in-south-africa-the-state-of-payshap-in-2026), [Stitch — what is PayShap/RPP](https://stitch.money/blog/what-is-payshap-the-rapid-payments-programme-in-south-africa), [Ozow — PayShap Request](https://ozow.com/our-products/instant-payments-with-payshap-request), [Peach Payments — PayShap Guide](https://support.peachpayments.com/support/solutions/articles/47001285345-payshap-guide), [News24 — PayShap limits increased](https://www.news24.com/business/companies/payshap-transaction-limits-have-increased-20241108), [WriteNow Agency — PayShap RTP for SA businesses](https://writenowagency.com/blog/bypassing-gateway-fees-payshap-request-to-pay-apis-for-sa-businesses).

## 2. Each major bank's own merchant/business payment API — verified

| Bank | What exists | Real programmatic confirmation? | Realistic access for TTLI |
|---|---|---|---|
| **Standard Bank** | Developer portal (developer.standardbank.co.za) with Payment/Collection APIs; a Precium partnership for collections + DebiCheck + reconciliation; a "Rapid Payments Platform" for corporate/investment banking clients (PayShap at scale) | Yes, but built for expected/recurring corporate collections, not a small-merchant checkout flow | Via Corporate &amp; Investment Banking relationship, not self-serve signup |
| **Absa** | Absa Access developer portal (developer.absa.africa); **Absa Pay** (launched 2024, built with Stitch and Ozow); an **EFT API** that lets a customer authorize payment via their own banking app instead of sharing credentials | Yes — described explicitly as reducing fraud versus manual EFT | Available to Business/Corporate Banking accountholders on a **tiered, volume-linked pricing model** — a commercial relationship, not a public API key |
| **FNB** | An "Integration Channel" for host-to-host/DebiCheck services; and, very recently (dated late July/early August 2026, i.e. essentially now), a new **direct API partnership with Ozow and RMB** giving Ozow merchants OAuth-authenticated, real-time payment straight through FNB/RMB rails, explicitly "replacing traditional EFT" | Yes, real-time, OAuth-authenticated | Practically accessed **through Ozow**, not FNB's own portal directly |
| **Nedbank** | A public API Marketplace (apim.nedbank.co.za) — an EFT Payments API, a Business Transactions API for reconciliation automation, and an AVS Real-Time account-verification API | Yes | Self-serve OAuth2 developer portal exists, more accessible in principle than the others, but oriented at treasury/reconciliation use cases |

The consistent pattern across all five: every major SA bank now has *some* real-time payment-initiation-with-confirmation capability, but for a business TTLI's size the practical door in every case is a PSP/aggregator (Ozow, Peach, Netcash, Stitch, Electrum), not a direct self-serve relationship with the bank's own developer portal. The direct portals are real but sales-assisted and volume-tiered.

## 3. "Instant EFT" products — verified, and this directly answers the task's core question

Confirmed for Ozow, PayFast, Peach Payments, iKhokha, and Netcash: modern Instant EFT is **not** just a smoother buyer experience that leaves TTLI still verifying manually. The buyer is authenticated either via a redirect into their own bank's real online-banking session/app (OTP or biometric) or, increasingly, via a proper OAuth-based bank API (the new FNB/RMB/Ozow deal specifically replaces the older credential-relay pattern with this). Either way, the **merchant receives a real-time webhook** — for PayFast this is the same ITN (Instant Transaction Notification) mechanism already implemented in TTLI's own `payfast.py`. iKhokha and PayFast both explicitly describe this as real-time-verified funds, not a promise.

Fee structures found: iKhokha quotes 2% for Instant EFT vs. 2.85% for local cards. PayFast doesn't publish a flat rate ("negotiable based on volume") but multiple sources describe Instant EFT as generally cheaper than card. PayFast has **no setup/monthly fee** and is described as the easiest onboarding of the major SA gateways for a small business — relevant since Payfast is already TTLI's named/coded card gateway.

**Transaction ceiling — this is the other structurally important finding:** Instant EFT is *not* bound by PayShap's hard R50,000 scheme cap. Its effective ceiling is the buyer's own bank's daily internet-banking transfer limit and the provider's own settings — sources describe limits from R100,000 and up being routinely seen. This makes Instant EFT (not PayShap) the structurally better fit for TTLI's Team and Corporate tiers, which PayShap's cap can't reach.

**Architecture fit — directly answers what the task asked about `services/payments/base.py`:** Because PayFast's hosted checkout page already presents Instant EFT as one of several payment-method tabs (alongside card) behind the *same* redirect and the *same* ITN webhook, this very plausibly requires **no new methods on the `PaymentProvider` Protocol at all** — `initiate_checkout`, `verify_signature`, `confirm_with_provider`, and `parse_webhook` as already written in `payfast.py` may handle it verbatim, the buyer simply picks a different tab on Payfast's own page. This is not fully certain from documentation alone (worth 30 minutes in the Payfast sandbox once live credentials exist to confirm the ITN payload is genuinely method-agnostic) but it is the single most concrete "cheap win" in this research. PayShap RTP, by contrast, is a **different integration shape**: it needs TTLI to collect the buyer's ShapID/phone number in its own UI (not a redirect), fire the request server-side via a PSP API, and asynchronously await a webhook while the order sits in a new intermediate state — closer to the request/webhook shape than the redirect shape, and would need a new `order_status` enum value (there's no `eft_rtp_pending`-equivalent in `docs/02_DATA_MODEL.md` §3 today) plus a new Protocol method, not a drop-in.

Sources: [PayFast — Instant EFT](https://payfast.io/features/payment-methods/instant-eft/), [PayFast — Payment Methods](https://payfast.io/features/payment-methods/), [iKhokha — Instant EFT on iK Pay Gateway](https://help.ikhokha.com/instant-eft-on-ik-pay-gateway), [Ozow, FNB and RMB API payments (ITWeb)](https://www.itweb.co.za/article/ozow-fnb-and-rmb-plug-into-api-payments/GxwQD71DPwAvlPVo), [Netcash — Instant EFT via Ozow](https://netcash.co.za/services/payment-gateway/payment-methods/instant-eft/).

## 4. Open Banking in South Africa — verified

Confirmed: South Africa has **no UK-style regulatory Open Banking mandate today (August 2026)**, but it is genuinely in motion, not absent. SARB's "Payments Ecosystem Modernisation" programme is opening the National Payment System to licensed non-banks; a draft activity-based authorisation framework covering eight payment-service categories (including fast-payment-scheme participation, i.e. PayShap) was out for public comment until June 15, 2026, with actual opening to non-bank providers targeted for the second half of 2026. In other words, as of today this is still a regulatory-drafting process, not a regime TTLI could formally apply into.

What exists *today* in its place is voluntary, commercial bank-fintech partnership — functionally similar but contractual rather than a legal right: Capitec Pay's ~28 TPPP partners, Absa Pay (built with Stitch/Ozow), FNB's new deal with Ozow/RMB. Stitch's own commentary is explicit that these bank-specific APIs are "practical implementations of open banking principles" rather than true interoperable open banking (Capitec Pay in particular is described as a closed loop, usable only by Capitec accountholders as payers).

**Accessibility to a business TTLI's size:** confirmed genuinely accessible. Stitch offers a self-serve product, Stitch Express (express.stitch.money), with Shopify/WooCommerce plugins and a self-serve API — "start accepting payments in as little as one day," explicitly positioned to give smaller e-commerce businesses the same payment infrastructure as large retailers. Ozow and PayFast similarly onboard small merchants directly (PayFast: no minimum turnover found, easiest onboarding of the group; Peach Payments: more rigorous, ~a week of back-and-forth on FICA/business docs, more oriented at R200k+/month stores). None of this requires TTLI to become a registered payment service provider itself — TTLI would onboard as an ordinary merchant client, going through FICA/KYB paperwork comparable in effort to any payment-gateway signup, which is not materially different from the onboarding TTLI will already do for Payfast card processing.

Sources: [Facephi — SA National Payment System: PSP licences and 2026 payments reform](https://facephi.com/observatory/en/national-payment-system-south-africa-psp-licences/), [Stitch — Open banking in South Africa](https://stitch.money/blog/open-banking-in-south-africa-payshap-capitec-pay-and-the-importance-of-bank-tppp-partnerships), [Stitch Express](https://stitch.money/), [Daily Maverick — Stitch adds BNPL for SA merchants](https://www.dailymaverick.co.za/article/2026-05-06-stitch-adds-buy-now-pay-later-for-south-african-merchants/), [openbankingtracker.com — South Africa](https://www.openbankingtracker.com/country/south-africa).

## 5. What TTLI's own bank could offer — synthesized guidance, open question flagged

Which bank TTLI banks with is unknown and should be confirmed with TTLI directly — not guessed. But the research above shows the practical question is the same regardless of which of the five it turns out to be: every major SA bank (Standard Bank, Absa, FNB, Nedbank, Capitec) now has *some* business-banking product in this space — a collections/reconciliation API (Standard Bank's Precium partnership, Nedbank's Business Transactions/AVS APIs), a payment-initiation-with-confirmation API (Absa's EFT API, Capitec Pay), or a PSP partnership that plugs straight into their rails (FNB via Ozow/RMB). In every case, though, these are business/corporate-banking-tier products reached through a relationship banker and volume-tiered commercial pricing, not a public self-serve signup. Concretely worth doing: a zero-cost conversation with TTLI's relationship banker asking specifically about (a) a "collections" or "payment notification" product for expected/reference-matched payments, and (b) whether PayShap Business/Request-to-Pay is available directly on the business account. This is worth having in parallel with any engineering decision, but shouldn't block one, since the PSP-mediated route (Section 3) is available regardless of the answer.

## 6. Effort vs. benefit — the recommendation

**Volume reality.** TTLI is pre-revenue at the platform level (`docs/05_COMMERCIAL.md`: "Nothing is sellable before Phase 4... a working checkout demo... will look like a finished business"). At pilot scale — tens to low hundreds of orders — and with EFT being a minority payment method even within that (Individual tiers default to card; EFT is the default only for Team/Corporate/Enterprise, which are inherently lower-volume, higher-touch B2B sales), the actual number of EFT approvals finance handles per month is very plausibly single digits to low dozens. A human glancing at a proof image and clicking approve is a 2–5 minute task. That is not, today, a backlog — it's normal manual review at the volume this system currently supports. `docs/02_DATA_MODEL.md` §12.4's own "EFT ageing alert" (&gt;48h pending) is the correct, already-planned signal for when this stops being true — that's the trigger to revisit this research, not a calendar date.

**Fraud-value reframe, worth being explicit about.** The task's framing was that manual review has real anti-fraud value that automation would give up. Having read `submit_proof`/`approve_eft` closely: the actual value of human review isn't really "confirming money moved" — it's confirming the `payment_reference` and amount in an uploaded image genuinely correspond to a real bank statement line. A human eyeballing a screenshot is not a stronger check than a webhook the merchant's own PaymentProvider Protocol verifies with a signature check plus a live server-to-server confirmation round-trip (`confirm_with_provider`, already built and documented in `payfast.py` as deliberately "fail closed" on any doubt) — that pattern is *more* rigorous against a forged-proof attack than a person's visual judgment, not less. So automating EFT confirmation via a properly-verified webhook is not really trading fraud protection for convenience; done with the same rigor TTLI already applied to the card path, it's closer to an upgrade. The genuine fraud consideration is different: South Africa has no UK-style mandatory reimbursement regime for authorised-push-payment fraud, so instant/irrevocable rails carry real scam risk — but that risk falls on the *payer* being tricked into paying the wrong party, not on TTLI as the receiving merchant confirming a real, correctly-referenced payment into its own real account. It doesn't change TTLI's own risk calculus.

**Prioritized recommendation, sequenced:**

1. **Do first, and it's already blocking, not new work:** get Payfast to a live sandbox/production account. This is Phase 0's own outstanding item (`payfast.py`'s docstring: "never executed against a live Payfast account... no sandbox merchant_id/merchant_key on file"), independent of this research, and everything below depends on it.
2. **Lowest-effort real win, do once step 1 is live:** confirm in the Payfast sandbox whether Instant EFT rides the same hosted-checkout redirect and ITN webhook already coded in `services/payments/payfast.py` and `services/payments/base.py`. If so — and the product literature strongly suggests it does — enabling it is a merchant-dashboard setting plus a UI option, reusing the existing `PaymentProvider` Protocol with **no new methods and likely no data-model change**, since it can plausibly follow `checkout_card`'s existing "stay at `pending_payment`, let the webhook resolve it" pattern rather than needing a new intermediate order status. This gives TTLI real programmatic EFT confirmation for the Individual and smaller-Team segment essentially for free, engineering-wise. This is the standout recommendation if only one thing gets picked up now.
3. **Defer until the EFT ageing alert actually starts firing regularly:** PayShap Request-to-Pay via the same PSP (Ozow, Netcash, or Peach all offer it; cheap, near-flat fees). This is a genuinely new feature, not a drop-in — new UI to capture the buyer's ShapID/phone, a new `order_status` enum value plus migration, and a new Protocol method shaped around request/webhook rather than redirect. Worth it once volume justifies the build, and only reaches the Individual/small-Team segment given the R50,000 scheme cap — it will never reach Corporate or Enterprise deals.
4. **Not worth pursuing at this scale, and possibly never as a "checkout automation" problem:** direct bank API integration (Standard Bank/Absa/Nedbank/FNB's own developer portals) or seeking a bank/PSP license under SA's still-drafting Open Banking framework. These carry onboarding, KYB, and ongoing-compliance cost that only pays off at real corporate transaction volume, and the PSP-mediated route in items 2–3 already captures most of the realistic benefit at a fraction of the cost. Enterprise-tier deals (R195,000–R550,000+) are invoiced, 30-day-term, negotiated contracts by design — automation of a "checkout" step doesn't really apply to them regardless of payment rail, so manual EFT/PO handling there is not a gap to close, it's the correct process.

**Bottom line:** manual EFT is genuinely fine at TTLI's current and near-term scale. The one low-effort, high-confidence move is confirming Instant EFT works through the Payfast integration TTLI is already building, once that integration goes live — everything past that should wait for the ageing-alert signal TTLI has already designed into the system to say so.
